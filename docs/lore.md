# Vareth and the Godbridle

The six peoples share **Vareth**, a continent wide enough that most people know their neighbors through border trade, old treaties, and stories told by soldiers. Their territories have stood for centuries. Then a mining crew brings the **Godbridle** to the surface, and everyone learns how much of that peace depended on the god beneath them staying asleep.

## The six peoples of Vareth

| People | Homeland | What they want from the Godbridle |
|---|---|---|
| **Druids** | The **Cairnspine**, a chain of windswept uplands where the oldest standing stones mark places the god can hear. Druids are born with an ear for its dreams. Tending the stones is a calling within their society, and most Druids live by herding, farming, or trade. | Return the Godbridle to its vault. Their oldest records describe the centuries it took the god to settle after its last command. Some Druids buried those records because they also reveal who gave the order. |
| **Elves** | The **Moonwash Coast**, a string of island cities and terraced ports. The Elves keep detailed maps of harbors, including entire districts now below the tide line. | Raise their drowned ancestral coast. The oldest living Elves remember walking its streets. Its ruins are now home to fishing towns whose livelihood would vanish if the sea withdrew. |
| **Humans** | The **Seven Marches**, a belt of grain country and fortified river towns. Rival crowns share the same river and have fought over its headwaters for generations. | Move the headwaters back toward their drying farms. The crown that found the Godbridle promises water to every March that recognizes its rule. |
| **Orcs** | The **Red Basin**, a broad interior of salt flats and grasslands. Orc towns hold seasonal rights to wells and pasture, and their assemblies meet wherever the water is good that year. | Bring back the inland sea. Its old shore is still visible in the cliffs. Filling the basin would restore its rivers and grasslands, and inundate some of the permanent Orc towns built there in the centuries since. |
| **Dryads** | The **Deepwood**, where each Dryad is born bound to a living tree. They can travel for years, but they feel injury to their tree across any distance. Dryad communities have carried seeds across Vareth to establish new groves. | Reconnect their scattered groves to the Deepwood. Roots could cross the continent in a single season under the god’s command. Some Dryads see that as the only way to save isolated groves, while others have spent generations building a life with their neighbors. |
| **Undead** | The **Still Kingdom**, a country of walled cities and catacombs. Its people keep their memories after death through funeral rites that bind them to a second body. Their first sovereigns still sit in the capital. | Ask the god to remember the dead who never received those rites. The oldest Undead want their families back. Some of the people they mean to summon died before the present borders existed and still have claims to the lands they left. |

## The sleeping god and its bridle

**Orrun** sleeps beneath Vareth. Rivers, forests, and fault lines shift as it dreams. The Godbridle lets someone speak a command into that dream. Orrun can spend years carrying it out in its sleep.

The mining crew tests the bridle by asking for water. By morning, a spring is running through their camp. A hundred miles away, the river feeding the Red Basin has run dry. Both events are well documented. It takes weeks for anyone to learn they happened on the same night.

**Who has the right to restore their homeland when doing so means remaking somebody else’s?** The Druids are already implicated. Long ago, their ancestors used the bridle to *make the mountains hold*. The Cairnspine rose, the Elven coast sank, and the Red Basin lost its sea. Several peoples now want to reverse the same ancient command, and several others depend on the continent it made.

## The five-year war

In **Year One**, a Human crown takes custody of the bridle. An Orc delegation follows its dry river upstream to demand the water’s return. The Druids arrive with a map that explains what happened and try to get the miners out before the armies close in.

The first shared battle takes place at the mine. A Human commander must evacuate the bridle, an Orc commander fights to capture the crew who used it, and a Druid commander tries to hold the tunnels long enough to get the workers out.

Faction campaigns enter the war at different points and can revisit the same battle from opposing sides. An Elven campaign could open in **Year Two** with a harbor rising out of the sea while its ships are still moored. An Undead campaign could begin in **Year Three** when a village starts hearing the voices of its unburied dead. Each campaign reveals a different consequence of the same struggle. By **Year Five**, the factions know enough to give Orrun a command deliberately, and each has people it would keep safe at someone else’s expense.
