# Vareth and the Godbridle

The six peoples share **Vareth**, a continent where most people know their neighbors through border trade, old treaties, and stories told by soldiers. Their territories have stood for centuries, but the peace between them is fraying. Old warnings about the **Godbridle** begin to matter again before anyone can say where it is. The fighting at the **Greyhollow mines** happens partway through the war, not at its beginning.

## The six peoples of Vareth

See [The leaders of Vareth](leaders.md) for each people's government, beliefs, and wartime leadership. These are their stakes over the course of the war, not what all six peoples know at the outset.

| People | Homeland | What is at stake for them |
|---|---|---|
| **Druids** | The **Cairnspine**, a chain of windswept uplands where the oldest standing stones mark places the god can hear. Druids are born with an ear for its dreams. Tending the stones is a calling, and most Druids live by herding, farming, or trade. | Return the Godbridle to its vault. Their oldest records describe the centuries it took the god to settle after its last command. Some Druids buried those records because they also reveal who gave the order. |
| **Elves** | The **Moonwash Coast**, a string of island cities and terraced ports. The Elves keep detailed maps of harbors, including entire districts now below the tide line. | Some ports want their drowned ancestral coast raised. The oldest living Elves remember walking its streets. Its ruins are now home to fishing towns whose livelihood would vanish if the sea withdrew. |
| **Humans** | The **Seven Marches**, a belt of grain country and fortified river towns. Rival crowns share the same river and have fought over its headwaters for generations. | At Greyhollow, King Mara Halden's detachment is sent to save miners, not to find an artifact. After taking custody of what they found, Mara promises to move the headwaters back toward the drying farms of every March that recognizes his rule. |
| **Orcs** | The **Red Basin**, a broad interior of salt flats and grasslands. Orc towns hold seasonal rights to wells and pasture, and their assemblies meet wherever the water is good that year. | Recover water lost after the discovery. Some want the whole inland sea back; its old shore is still visible in the cliffs. Restoring it would also inundate permanent Orc towns built there in the centuries since. |
| **Dryads** | The **Deepwood**, where each Dryad is born bound to a living tree. They can travel for years, but they feel injury to their tree across any distance. Dryad communities have carried seeds across Vareth to establish new groves. | Neris Alder and the Dryads who follow her to Greyhollow believe the world will end if Humans reach the Godbridle. They fight to stop that from happening. Later, as isolated groves lose water, some Dryads consider using the bridle to reconnect them to the Deepwood; a crossing would also run through lands their neighbors depend on. |
| **Undead** | The **Still Kingdom**, a country of walled cities and catacombs. Its people keep their memories after death through funeral rites that bind them to a second body. Their first sovereigns still sit in the capital. | Ask the god to remember the dead who never received those rites. The oldest Undead want their families back. Some of the people they mean to summon died before the present borders existed and still have claims to lands they left. |

## Orrun, the bridle, and the warning

**Orrun** sleeps beneath Vareth. Rivers, forests, and fault lines shift as it dreams. The Godbridle lets someone speak a command to Orrun. A command can rouse it without ending its dreams, and the work can continue for years. Long ago, the Druids' ancestors used the bridle to *make the mountains hold*. The Cairnspine rose, the Elven coast sank, and the Red Basin lost its sea. Several peoples want to reverse that command; several others depend on the continent it made.

Before Greyhollow, the Dryads have a prophecy, old accounts of what Orrun's power can do, and reason to think the mines may be where the bridle lies. They do not have proof that it is there, or that Human contact with it will end the world. Neris believes the warning and acts on it. The ancient damage is history, not proof that her reading of the prophecy is right.

Mara's detachment saves the miners during the Dryad attack. Only after the rescue, the workers tell the detachment what they found: the Godbridle. They later test it by asking for water. Orrun wakes enough to answer; a spring breaks through their camp and the river feeding the Red Basin goes dry the same night. Neither side knows both effects at first. Mara takes custody, and testimony later connects the spring and the dry river. The disaster is real, but it does not establish what fulfilling the prophecy would mean.

**Who has the right to restore their homeland when doing so means remaking somebody else's?** Finding the Godbridle changes the war from a fight partly shaped by warnings and guesses into a contest over who can give Orrun an order, who would pay for it, and whether anyone should give one at all.
