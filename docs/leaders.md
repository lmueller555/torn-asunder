# The leaders of Vareth

Each of the six peoples has a leader who can send armies to war. What differs is **who gave them that authority, what they owe their people, and who can take it away**.

## Druids — First Listener Sova Cairn

Sova has the Cairnspine Druids’ slate-gray skin, layered brow ridges, and pale eyes. He ties his white beard in braids weighted with stone beads.

The Cairnspine is governed through its standing stones. Each valley chooses a Keeper who settles disputes over shared land and water. When a matter concerns Orrun, the Keepers name a First Listener to interpret its dreams and speak for them. Sova can close a sacred site and call its guards to defend it. Sending those guards beyond the Cairnspine requires the consent of their home valleys.

Druids believe that every command given to Orrun leaves a debt someone will have to pay. Their oath ceremonies require a witness to name who might bear that cost. Sova’s predecessors kept the record of the ancient command that sank the Elven coast under seal. He intends to read it aloud at the next gathering, knowing some Keepers may walk out before he can ask them to help recover the Godbridle.

## Elves — Tide Speaker Ilyra Venn

Ilyra has the long, narrow ears and luminous sea-glass eyes of the coastal Elves. She wears her silver hair swept back above wave-etched armor.

The Moonwash Coast is a league of independent ports. Every twelve years, in a fixed rotation, a different port appoints the Tide Speaker. The Speaker sets foreign policy and commands ships pledged to the league. Each port keeps control of its own docks and decides how much of its fleet to pledge.

Elves treat firsthand memory as public evidence. Harbor courts still call witnesses to testify about streets that have been underwater for centuries. Ilyra grew up on the drowned coast and can name the families whose homes she wants to raise. She can also name the fishing villages that would lose their harbors if she succeeds. One of those villages now supplies the pilots her fleet depends on.

## Humans — King Mara Halden

Mara wears a narrow gold crown above close-cropped dark hair and a trimmed beard. His rust-red mantle identifies him with the Marches.

The Seven Marches have seven hereditary crowns. Mara rules the March where the Godbridle was found and holds it under his own royal seal. He can muster his own army immediately. Every other crown that joins him can bargain for water, marriage rights, or command of part of the host.

Humans of the Marches swear important oaths at wells and river crossings. A ruler’s first duty is to keep water reaching the people downstream, including across a rival’s border. Mara spent the last drought buying grain from crowns that now want a share of the bridle. He promises water to any March that recognizes his rule. His own judges are already hearing petitions from farmers who ask whether he has the right to make that offer.

## Orcs — War Marshal Raska Vennor

Raska has the Orcs’ olive-green skin, broad jaw, and prominent lower tusks. He wears a red well-keeper’s scarf over heavy iron and leather.

Orc towns send delegates to a seasonal assembly that meets wherever the Red Basin has reliable water. The assembly grants grazing and well rights and elects a marshal for a named campaign. Raska’s mandate is to restore the river. A town can recall its company if he commits it to another cause.

In the Basin, a household that maintains a well earns the first draw. After that, it is expected to share with travelers. Orc families recite the wells they have kept open when they bring a claim before the assembly. Raska earned his standing by keeping a chain of wells open through two failed rains. His authority starts to fray when the lower towns ask him to bring back the entire inland sea. The shoreline they want would run through the town hosting the assembly.

## Dryads — Rootspeaker Neris Alder

Neris’s face grows in thin plates of living wood. Root fibers braid down her neck, and young leaves grow from the branches above her amber eyes.

The groves govern through living root networks. When a grove joins the Deepwood, any Dryad bound to one of its trees can send assent or a refusal through the roots. The Rootspeaker can direct a common defense, but a new root crossing requires the assent of every grove it would join. Isolated groves speak for themselves through travelers and seed envoys.

Dryads mark adulthood by planting a tree under whose shade they may never sit. When they establish a grove far from the Deepwood, they ask their neighbors to witness the planting and record the terms under which the grove may grow. Neris wants to use the Godbridle to save groves whose trees are failing. The oldest of them has sent back a single instruction. Its people want her help keeping their promise to the town that took them in.

## Undead — High Executor Sereth Vale

Sereth wears the Still Kingdom’s mourning hood and silver burial collar. Two points of violet light are visible in the empty sockets of his skull.

The Still Kingdom is governed under the Last Testament, a charter whose original signatories still sit on its highest court. They appoint the High Executor to lead armies and act on the court’s rulings. Each city administers its own funeral rites. An order to recall the dead across the whole kingdom would need a ruling on who can authorize a return when the dead left no recorded wishes.

The Undead keep an empty place at family tables for anyone whose death passed without rites. In some houses the place has been set for centuries. Sereth helped write the requirement that a person must consent to return. His younger sister died before the rites existed, and he has spent two centuries looking for a letter that might tell him what she would have wanted. Now the court has asked him to secure the Godbridle before another people gives Orrun a command that could put the question beyond its reach.

## The first confrontation

At the start of the war, **Mara holds the bridle, Raska has a mandate to recover the water, and Sova has the evidence that could bring the other peoples into the conflict**. Their first confrontation at the mine turns on three different orders. Mara’s commander must get the bridle out. Raska’s must bring back someone who can explain what happened to the river. Sova’s must get the miners to safety before anyone else can claim them.
