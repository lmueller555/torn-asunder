# The five-year war: shared history and campaign windows

This is the story team's spoiler reference for one history of Vareth. The campaigns follow different stretches of that history. A window marks the first and last events shown, not a chapter for every intervening year. A victory can change who gets out, what a leader learns, or whose help they win without giving a different answer about who held the Godbridle the next morning.

**Established:** [Vareth and the Godbridle](lore.md), [the six leaders](leaders.md), and the [Year One mine battle](combat.md#year-one-battle-at-the-mine) establish Orrun, the miners' request for water, the three opening orders at the mine, and each faction's reason to intervene. The Campaign Vault already sets Ilyra's opening at a rising harbor in Year Two and Sereth's at a village hearing its dead in Year Three. This document fixes what connects those events, where each campaign ends, and the outcome they all share. It does not specify individual missions.

## Shared history

### Year One: The spring and the dry river

When the miners ask Orrun for water, a spring breaks through their camp and the river feeding the Red Basin runs dry. King Mara Halden claims the Godbridle and has it carried out of the mine through the pass. First Listener Sova Cairn gets the workers out through the tunnels. War Marshal Raska Vennor, sent to bring back someone who can explain the loss, meets one of the evacuated miners at the outlet. She goes with him voluntarily after he agrees to let the rest leave. Her account and Sova's survey date both changes to the same night. Mara keeps the artifact; he cannot keep the discovery secret.

### Year Two: The harbor rises

Mara's allies are ready to break with him over a second bad harvest. Rather than lose their grain and troops, he asks Orrun to send water back into the Marches' dry headwater channels. Orrun tips the coastal riverbed to make the water run inland. At Moonwash, old harbor walls rise under the moored ships, leaving some stranded on stone. Water reaches part of the Marches, but not all the farms Mara promised it to, and the displaced fishing fleet can no longer feed its own ports.

Tide Speaker Ilyra Venn uses the pledged fleet to haul crews and cargo off the stranded ships instead of sending it to seize the Godbridle. Sova reads the sealed record of the first Druid command aloud: raising the Cairnspine drowned that same coast and helped drain the Red Basin. Several Keepers walk out. Ilyra now has evidence for the ports that want the old coast restored, but she has just seen what one rising harbor does to the people living above it.

### Year Three: Voices from the cut

The diverted river cuts a new channel along the Still Kingdom's border. Where the bank falls away from its oldest graves, a village starts hearing voices address the living by name. No one can establish whether these are the dead themselves or their memories passing through Orrun's dream. The Still Kingdom's court sends High Executor Sereth Vale to secure the bridle before another command silences the voices, leaving the question of consent to a later ruling.

The same cut starves the oldest isolated Dryad grove of water. Rootspeaker Neris Alder wants to bring the Deepwood to its roots, but the grove withholds assent: the shortest crossing would run under the wells of the town that sheltered it. The town offers space for a nursery if the grove helps keep the old feeder channel open. Neris accepts the work while continuing to seek custody of the bridle as insurance for other groves.

### Year Four: The breach at Spanwater

At Spanwater Lock the new channel branches near the old Basin spillway. The lower Orc towns press Raska to widen the breach and fill the old sea. Raska opens the spillway into the Basin’s old riverbed but leaves the sea gate shut. Flooding the old seabed would swallow the town where the assembly meets. He secures water for the Basin; the lower towns recall their companies, and the Marches lose part of their new supply. Neris keeps the town's feeder channel open during the release. Ilyra carries stranded households out by water and refuses the ports' order to pursue the bridle convoy with the same ships. Those ports withdraw their ships once the households are clear.

Mara refuses to ask Orrun to close the breach and releases grain to an unpledged March that lost water, weakening his coalition further. Rather than risk a rival crown taking the bridle, he hands it to Sova in sight of Orc and Elven witnesses. The handover changes custody, not ownership by treaty: the rival crowns and the Still Kingdom still mean to claim it before it reaches the vault.

### Year Five: The vault closes

At the Cairnspine, Sova needs valley consent to guard the road to the vault. He wins enough Keepers by reading the full record of Druid responsibility into the terms of custody, so that no First Listener can seal it away again. The Dryad groves refuse a continent-wide crossing; Neris brings local volunteers to keep refugees and witnesses on the road even as rival claimants advance. Sereth finds his sister's letter among the village's recovered records. She asks him to leave a lamp in the window after she dies; she says nothing about returning. He refuses to escort the court's speaker to the vault and protects the witnesses instead. The court strips him of his commission.

The bridle goes back into the vault without a new command. Orc, Elven, and Human witnesses attest that Sova closed it without claiming the right to use it. The rival crowns lose their last avenue to a quick seizure, and the court recalls its remaining forces rather than send them alone against the Keepers. That ends the war over possession, not the consequences: Orrun is still carrying out Mara's order, the Basin has water but not its sea, the raised harbor stays raised, and the groves and displaced households still have to work out where to live. The old command is not erased and there is no universal recall of the dead.

## Six campaign windows

| Campaign | Opens on | Ends on |
|---|---|---|
| **Humans · Mara Halden · Years One–Two** | The mine escort. Mara knows the Basin lost its river, but thinks a precise order can spare other peoples and keep the Marches allied. | The ships stranded at Moonwash and the first reports of which Marches actually got water. He will not give a second order to correct the first. The Year Four handover is seen from other campaigns, not played again as Mara. |
| **Orcs · Raska Vennor · Years One–Four** | The mine interception. The delegation needs a witness its assembly will believe; seizing the artifact alone would prove nothing. | Spanwater. Raska restores the river he was elected to recover, rejects the sea demanded by the lower towns, and loses their companies. |
| **Druids · Sova Cairn · Years One–Five** | The tunnel evacuation. Sova knows the records his predecessors hid, but cannot order guards beyond the Cairnspine on his own. The campaign moves from his Year Two disclosure to the Year Four handover; it does not play the year of the voices. | The return to the vault. He gains the right to guard it by making the Druids' debt public, not by persuading the others that the old continent was better. |
| **Elves · Ilyra Venn · Years Two–Four** | The harbor rising under her fleet. Ilyra can name the streets below deck and the fishing villages her pilots need. | Spanwater. She spends the fleet's time on evacuation, not on winning leverage to raise the coast, and must return to ports that no longer all back her. |
| **Dryads · Neris Alder · Years Three–Five** | The failing grove. Neris learns that people she means to save would rather risk transplanting their trees than break their promise to the town. | The road to the vault. Nearby groves choose to work together while keeping their right to refuse a root crossing; no one is bound by a command spoken on their behalf. |
| **Undead · Sereth Vale · Years Three–Five** | The village hearing its dead. A voice is reason to listen, but not permission to bring someone back. Sereth still hopes to find his sister's letter. | The bid for the vault. Sereth upholds the consent requirement he helped write even when it costs him his commission and the chance to ask Orrun for his sister back. |

## Where perspectives can overlap

- **The mine (Year One; Humans, Orcs, Druids).** The artifact leaves by the pass; the workers leave by the tunnels on the same map. The Orcs' initial order is to capture witnesses, but a freely given account meets its purpose. The three victories do not require the bridle to change hands or the whole crew to be both captured and evacuated.
- **Spanwater (Year Four; Orcs, Elves, Dryads).** Raska needs the spillway open only to the river level. Ilyra needs boats clear of the rising water; Neris needs the town's feeder channel to carry water after the release. None needs to undo the breach, and the first battle need not reveal the outcome of either rescue or irrigation work. Sova's campaign can pick up the custody transfer without replaying the clash.
- **The Cairnspine road (Year Five; Druids, Dryads, Undead).** Sova protects the bridle and gets it into the vault; Neris keeps civilians and witnesses moving; Sereth has to prevent his own court from speaking for unconsenting dead. All three can meet those goals without making each player deliver or claim the same artifact.

A briefing can report a faction's inference as a belief, not as a hidden fact. In particular, neither hearing the dead nor recognizing a voice resolves the question of consent. Show another view of a battle only if it changes what the player is trying to accomplish or what they learn; otherwise move on to that leader's next consequential decision.

Structural precedent only: [Blueside's description of *Kingdom Under Fire: Heroes*](https://www.blueside.co.kr/ENG/games/kufheroes.asp) describes separate commanders' campaigns pursuing different events within a shared war. The events and characters above belong to Vareth.
