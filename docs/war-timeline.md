# The five-year war: shared history and campaign windows

This is the story team's spoiler reference for one history of Vareth. The campaigns follow different stretches of that history. A window marks the first and last events shown, not a chapter for every intervening year. A victory can change who gets out, what a leader learns, or whose help they win without giving a different answer about who held the Godbridle the next morning.

[Vareth and the Godbridle](lore.md) establishes the public premise. The years here count from the beginning of the war, not from the discovery. **Greyhollow in Year Three is the story's midpoint:** what was a warning with a possible location becomes a thing armies can possess and use. Campaign order in the menu need not match chronological order. Campaign and battlefield routing are tracked separately in the implementation index.

## Shared history

### Years One–Two: The warning before the find

Territorial and water disputes already divide Vareth. A drought threatens the isolated Dryad grove, and Neris Alder helps it start a nursery. This drought predates the Godbridle's discovery and the later river diversion. Neris hears the warning that Humans reaching the Godbridle will end the world and takes it literally. In Year Two, she surveys two routes at the Cairnspine Boundary Post. The roads and an ordinary carter's testimony point to Greyhollow as a possible site. No faction has confirmed the location or used the bridle in the present war, and Orrun is still asleep. The groves can fear the danger while disagreeing about how to respond.

### Year Three: Greyhollow, then Orrun's awakening

The Dryads attack the Greyhollow mines to prevent the Humans from reaching the Godbridle. The detachment King Mara Halden sends knows it must rescue the miners under attack. It is not told to escort, collect, or search for a relic. The detachment rallies at the south tunnel entrance; the miners flee south through it and then west to safety. The rescue succeeds, and the Dryads fail to prevent Human access. **Only after the miners are safe** does the Human campaign's victory cutscene, “What They Brought Out,” reveal that the crew found the Godbridle underground. The rescue and the Dryad finale show the same battle and the same outcome; neither version lets the Dryads seize the artifact or change history.

After the reveal, the miners ask Orrun for water. Orrun wakes enough to answer, a spring breaks through their camp, and the Red Basin loses the river that feeds it. When the consequences reach the other factions, Mara takes the bridle under his seal. First Listener Sova Cairn gets the workers away from the mine through its tunnels before new forces close in. War Marshal Raska Vennor, sent to find out why the Basin lost its river, meets them at the outlet. One miner volunteers to testify if the others can leave safely. Raska agrees. Her account and Sova's survey date both changes to the same night. This is a later encounter at Greyhollow, not a second account of the original Dryad attack. The workers' earlier rescue took them out of immediate danger; this departure gets them away from the mine altogether.

### Later in Year Three: The harbor rises

Mara's allies are ready to break with him over a second bad harvest. Rather than lose their grain and troops, he asks Orrun to send water back into the Marches' dry headwater channels. Orrun tips the coastal riverbed to make the water run inland. At Moonwash, old harbor walls rise under the moored ships, leaving some stranded on stone. Water reaches part of the Marches, but not all the farms Mara promised it to, and the displaced fishing fleet can no longer feed its own ports.

Tide Speaker Ilyra Venn uses the pledged fleet to haul crews and cargo off the stranded ships instead of sending it to seize the Godbridle. Sova reads the sealed record of the first Druid command aloud: raising the Cairnspine drowned that same coast and helped drain the Red Basin. Several Keepers walk out. Ilyra now has evidence for the ports that want the old coast restored, but she has just seen what one rising harbor does to the people living above it.

### Later in Year Three: Voices from the cut

The diverted river cuts a new channel along the Still Kingdom's border. Where the bank falls away from its oldest graves, a village starts hearing voices address the living by name. No one can establish whether these are the dead themselves or their memories passing through Orrun's dream. The Still Kingdom's court sends High Executor Sereth Vale to secure the bridle before another command silences the voices, leaving the question of consent to a later ruling.

The new channel cuts off water to the oldest isolated Dryad grove again. It still has the nursery the town offered before the discovery; now that nursery depends on keeping the old feeder channel open. The grove again refuses a root crossing that would run beneath the town’s wells. Neris agrees to help keep the feeder working. This is later shared history, not a playable chapter of the Dryad campaign. The danger she predicted has not come to pass as she understood it; the people facing the actual damage still have a say in any remedy.

### Year Four: The breach at Spanwater

At Spanwater Lock the new channel branches near the old Basin spillway. The lower Orc towns press Raska to widen the breach and fill the old sea. Raska opens the spillway into the Basin’s old riverbed but leaves the sea gate shut. Flooding the old seabed would swallow the town where the assembly meets. He secures water for the Basin; the lower towns recall their companies, and the Marches lose part of their new supply. Neris keeps the town's feeder channel open during the release. Ilyra carries stranded households out by water and refuses the ports' order to pursue the bridle convoy with the same ships. Those ports withdraw their ships once the households are clear.

Mara refuses to ask Orrun to close the breach and releases grain to an unpledged March that lost water, weakening his coalition further. Rather than risk a rival crown taking the bridle, he hands it to Sova in sight of Orc and Elven witnesses. The handover changes custody, not ownership by treaty: the rival crowns and the Still Kingdom still mean to claim it before it reaches the vault.

### Year Five: The vault closes

At the Cairnspine, Sova needs valley consent to guard the road to the vault. He wins enough Keepers by reading the full record of Druid responsibility into the terms of custody, so that no First Listener can seal it away again. The Dryad groves refuse a continent-wide crossing; Neris brings local volunteers to keep refugees and witnesses on the road even as rival claimants advance. Sereth finds his sister's letter among the village's recovered records. She asks him to leave a lamp in the window after she dies; she says nothing about returning. He refuses to escort the court's speaker to the vault and protects the witnesses instead. The court strips him of his commission.

The bridle goes back into the vault without a new command. Orc, Elven, and Human witnesses attest that Sova closed it without claiming the right to use it. The rival crowns lose their last avenue to a quick seizure, and the court recalls its remaining forces rather than send them alone against the Keepers. That ends the war over possession, not the consequences: Orrun is still carrying out Mara's order, the Basin has water but not its sea, the raised harbor stays raised, and the groves and displaced households still have to work out where to live. The old command is not erased and there is no universal recall of the dead.

Ilyra can name the streets below deck and the fishing villages her pilots need. | Spanwater. | [**Undead · Sereth Vale · Years Three–Five**](campaigns/undead.md) | The village hearing its dead, after Mara's command diverts the river. A voice is reason to listen, but not permission to bring someone back. Sereth still hopes to find his sister's letter. | The bid for the vault. ## Where perspectives can overlap

- **Greyhollow rescue (Year Three; Humans and Dryads).** The same camp, mine approaches, ridge, and pass serve both perspectives. The Humans get the miners to safety; the Dryads fail to stop them. The Human victory cutscene alone needs to show what the miners found. The Dryad briefing can name the prophecy and suspected site without confirming the find or requiring the player to watch the Human reveal early.
- **Greyhollow departure (later in Year Three; Orcs and Druids).** Reuse the mine and tunnel outlet after the water test. Raska secures a voluntarily given account; Sova gets the other workers away. The Human convoy can be in the background, with Mara already holding the bridle. Neither revised opening should treat interception of the Human rescue, prior knowledge of the artifact in the Human briefing, or clearing another campaign as a prerequisite.
- **Spanwater (Year Four; Orcs and Elves).** Raska needs the spillway open only to the river level. Ilyra needs boats clear of rising water; Neris keeps the town's feeder channel working as a shared story event. None needs to undo the breach. Sova's campaign can pick up the custody transfer without replaying the clash.
- **The Cairnspine road (Year Five; Druids and Undead).** Sova protects the bridle and gets it into the vault; Sereth prevents his court from speaking for unconsenting dead; Neris keeps civilians and witnesses moving in the shared history. No player has to complete a different faction's objectives to advance.

A briefing can report a faction's inference as a belief, not as a hidden fact. The Dryads' conviction is real, but it does not confirm their prophecy. Neither hearing the dead nor recognizing a voice resolves the question of consent. Show another view of a battle only if it changes what the player is trying to accomplish or what they learn.

Structural precedent only: [Blueside's description of *Kingdom Under Fire: Heroes*](https://www.blueside.co.kr/ENG/games/kufheroes.asp) describes separate commanders' campaigns pursuing different events within a shared war. The events and characters above belong to Vareth.
